## Design principles

### Make thinking the visual subject

The most important content is the learner's question, prediction, evidence judgment, design, changed model, and next questions. Decorative graphics must support that narrative rather than compete with it.

### Editorial, not dashboard-like

Use expressive serif headlines, disciplined sans-serif interface copy, generous breathing room, fine rules, and clear reading order. Avoid dense panels, uniform card grids, generic chat bubbles, and admin-dashboard chrome.

### Productive friction should feel intentional

Prediction is a gate, but the interface should make it feel like committing a field-note hypothesis—not completing a punitive form. Hint and Explain now are visible escape hatches after the attempt.

### Provenance is part of the design

Evidence, inference, open questions, sources, live/seeded mode, age boundary, AI fallibility, and the 24-hour browser-retention boundary are visible parts of the experience, not hidden legal text.

### The map transformation earns the drama

Keep most animation subtle so the final Curiosity Map expansion after reflection feels meaningful. The map is a learning trace, not decorative network art.

## Visual foundation

### Color roles

Use semantic roles rather than scattering literal values through components. Exact CSS values should be centralized in the application theme.

| Token role              | Visual intent              | Use                                                                                |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `canvas`                | Warm paper/off-white       | Page background                                                                    |
| `surface`               | Slightly brighter paper    | Inputs, route cards, Discovery Card                                                |
| `ink`                   | Deep maritime navy         | Primary text and dark-map ground                                                   |
| `muted-ink`             | Desaturated navy/gray      | Metadata, incomplete steps, supporting copy                                        |
| `teal`                  | Deep ocean teal            | Primary actions, active steps, completed trace                                     |
| `teal-soft`             | Pale blue-green            | Selected cards, focus halos, evidence accents                                      |
| `coral`                 | Warm coral/orange          | Decorative marks, open-question branches, and non-text emphasis                    |
| `--coral-text: #ad4728` | Dark accessible coral text | Meaningful coral text on paper surfaces; 4.98:1 on paper and 5.34:1 on paper-light |
| `line`                  | Sand/gray rule             | Dividers, borders, inactive controls                                               |
| `danger`                | Restrained red             | Errors only; never decorative                                                      |

The concept language uses warm paper, deep navy, ocean teal, and coral sparingly. Maintain strong contrast; never rely on color alone to communicate completion, evidence kind, or selection.

### Texture and illustration

- Fine topographic/ocean line texture may appear at very low contrast.
- Engraving-like science illustrations anchor canonical screens. The three current WebPs are optimized derivatives of project-specific OpenAI-generated originals whose source hashes, trusted C2PA validation, prompt summaries, and derivative hashes are recorded in `docs/route-art-provenance.json`.
- Illustration should not imply functionality or factual evidence.
- Keep text surfaces quiet; do not place detailed texture behind dense copy.
- Provide a clean solid-color fallback for reduced motion, high contrast, or asset failure.

### Typography

- **Display:** editorial serif for product wordmark, page headlines, and major Discovery/Card titles.
- **Interface:** highly legible humanist sans-serif for controls, body, metadata, citations, and map labels.
- Limit body line length to roughly 55–75 characters where possible.
- Use sentence case for buttons and labels.
- Avoid tiny uppercase tracking as the sole category cue.
- Generated content must not be allowed to shrink typography; truncate/summarize within documented bounds instead.

Suggested scale roles:

| Role          | Desktop intent         | Mobile intent |
| ------------- | ---------------------- | ------------- |
| Hero/display  | 56–72px, tight leading | 40–52px       |
| Page title    | 44–56px                | 34–42px       |
| Section title | 28–36px                | 26–32px       |
| Card title    | 22–30px                | 20–26px       |
| Body          | 16–18px                | 16–18px       |
| Metadata      | 14–16px                | 14–16px       |

Use responsive `clamp()` values and test real generated strings. These are targets, not permission to sacrifice fit or accessibility.

## Layout

### Desktop

- Maximum content width around 1440–1520px with generous side gutters.
- Spark: editorial statement/illustration beside the question form.
- Choose: three route columns or cards with enough room for method distinctions.
- Workspace: main learning content approximately two-thirds; compact evolving map approximately one-third.
- Discovery: map and Discovery Card side by side, with the changed-thinking statement above.

### Mobile

- Single semantic column.
- Replace the seven-label desktop stepper with a compact progress count and accessible menu/outline.
- Keep touch targets at least 44×44 CSS pixels.
- Stack map before Discovery Card so the transformation remains the narrative payoff.
- At 760px and below, default to the text outline from first paint; desktop defaults to the graphic. Preserve an explicit learner choice, make **View map** recenter the graphic, keep the outline keyboard-scrollable, and keep controls reachable.
- Keep Copy Markdown reachable without horizontal scrolling.

### Spacing

Use a consistent 4px-derived scale, with larger editorial jumps between narrative sections. Suggested tokens: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96. Dense metadata may use 8–12px gaps; primary sections should usually use 32–64px.

## Components

### Header and progress

- Wordmark anchors the left edge.
- Desktop shows the seven-step sequence with completed checkmarks and an underlined current step.
- Selecting the wordmark starts a new flow with confirmation when work is in progress; **Clear session** removes the learner's current quest and draft from this browser but intentionally leaves the separate random safety identifier until site data is cleared.
- Mobile uses a compact step count while each screen's focused heading supplies the current-step context.
- Icon-only mobile header controls retain explicit accessible names, and the ContextBar keeps the pre-generated/live provenance badge visible.

### Question input

- Large textarea with a visible `3–300 characters · no personal info` boundary and counter.
- Sample prompts are real buttons/chips with icons as secondary cues.
- Level and duration use segmented controls with text plus checked state.
- Primary **Generate 3 routes** and secondary **Try complete demo** remain visually distinct.
- Validation appears next to the field and does not erase input.

### Route card

Each of exactly three cards includes title, hook, lens, activity, estimated time, and a distinct icon/illustration cue. Its radio uses the route title as a distinct accessible name. Selected/hover/focus states must not cause layout shift. Make the whole card understandable before the action appears; do not hide essential metadata behind hover.

### Prediction panel

- Restate the driving question.
- Explain why a prediction is requested.
- Show a meaningful minimum without coercive grading language.
- Keep evidence visually unavailable until submission.
- After submission, show the saved prediction as a persistent reference.

### Evidence Lens

- Lead with: **Evidence is sourced. Inference is reasoned. Open questions stay open.**
- Use text labels and consistent icon/border treatments for the three kinds.
- Place source links directly with the supported evidence, showing title/domain and an external-link cue.
- Expand/collapse details without hiding the core claim or citation.
- Include an uncertainty note below the set when appropriate.

### Evidence Decision

- Place a distinct **Your evidence decision** panel between the Evidence Lens and creation challenge so the learner must interpret evidence before applying it.
- Ask the learner to select one current source-backed `Evidence` finding; do not offer inference or open-question items as decision targets.
- Show that selected finding's complete ordered linked source list beside the decision fields. Preserve title, domain, destination, and source order; do not collapse a multi-source finding to one representative link.
- Present **Supports my prediction**, **Challenges my prediction**, and **Complicates my prediction** as a native radio group with visible selected, focus, and error states. Never imply that one relationship is the correct answer.
- Require learner-authored establishes, unresolved, and impact fields (each 15–300 characters) plus the relationship; keep their counters and validation text adjacent to the fields. The persisted key remains `unresolved`, but learner-facing field 04 is **Where does this source scope stop?** with the help text **Separate what this source directly supports from your inference or a question it cannot answer.**
- Label the panel **Your judgment · not AI grading**. The visual hierarchy should distinguish this learner-owned reasoning from generated synthesis.
- Preserve the selected finding, relationship, judgment fields, exact sources, and source boundary in the final map, Discovery Card, and Markdown export.

### Evidence to design

- After the Evidence Decision and before reflection, show **Evidence → design** beside the creation work: the learner explains one concrete `designChoice` in 20–400 characters.
- The link must reuse the exact current sourced finding selected in the Evidence Decision. Changing that selected finding clears the design link and the creation self-check so the learner explicitly reconnects evidence to their work.
- Present the link as learner-authored reasoning, not model feedback or a correctness score.
- Preserve it as an `EvidenceApplication` in v4 session/draft storage, send it with reflection, and render it in the map, Discovery Card, and Markdown export.

### Creation and reflection

- Present constraints as a short readable list, not tags scattered around the page.
- Make completion criteria visible before typing.
- Explain that the versioned session and draft stay in this browser for a fixed maximum of 24 hours. Their first-save deadline does not slide with edits; expired work is removed while the app remains open or on the next load.
- Provide autosave/local persistence feedback without distracting toasts, and recommend **Clear session** on a shared device.
- Reflection fields retain the exact labels `I used to think…`, `Now I think…`, and `I still wonder…`.
- Specific feedback should be visually distinct from the learner's own text.

### Curiosity Map

- Dark navy field creates contrast with the warm paper experience.
- Completed/current nodes use teal; next questions use coral; unavailable/incomplete nodes use dashed neutral treatments.
- Use shape, label, and line style in addition to color.
- Final map contains roughly 6–10 semantic nodes and exactly three next-question branches.
- Every node must map to real session content.
- Provide **View as outline** as a first-class accessible fallback.
- Make the horizontally scrollable map viewport a labeled keyboard-focusable region.
- Never ask GPT-5.6 for pixel coordinates.

### Discovery Card

Lead with a compact **At a glance** section that always includes Before, Now, the selected finding and its exact linked sources, evidence/source boundary, design move, and the single selected `My next question`. Place the complete journey, learner reflection, and ReasonWeave response inside a native **Full learning trace** disclosure so the compact payoff stays readable without deleting evidence. Keep the optional facilitator discussion prompt and copy/download actions visible outside the disclosure. The decision content should name the relationship and learner judgment, and the separate evidence-to-design content should identify the selected finding and concrete design choice without presenting either as an AI grade. Card/export remain unavailable until exactly one of the three reflection questions is selected; that selection must not start another quest. Copy/download controls need explicit success state. Export must omit internal IDs, prompts, safety identifier, and provider metadata, escape authored raw HTML and active Markdown constructs, and normalize HTTP(S) source destinations before emitting links.

On the first Branch choice at viewport widths up to 930px, move focus to the named Discovery Card region and keep that region visible while its deferred chunk loads. Desktop and subsequent user choices retain focus on the selected radio; restored selections do not auto-focus the card. Announce the completed transition with the exact status **Card unlocked. Map updated.**

### Mode and system states

- Seeded mode uses a persistent `Pre-generated demo quest` badge.
- Persistent privacy copy states that learner work stays in this browser for up to 24 hours, should not contain personal information, and should be cleared on a shared device.
- Loading states explain the action (`Finding three routes`, `Checking sources`) rather than saying only `Thinking…`.
- Preserve content in errors and offer **Try again** plus **Open complete demo** where relevant.
- Timeouts, empty states, and unavailable citations have specific copy.
- In production/no-key Spark, the complete pre-generated demo is the only primary action; disable live inputs, let Enter start the demo without a routes request, and clearly state when exploration is unavailable because both paths are disabled.

## Interaction and motion

- Route cards may enter with a restrained stagger.
- Step changes may use short fades/slides that preserve reading position.
- Evidence reveals only after prediction submission.
- The evidence-to-design chain remains incomplete until the learner has recorded a sourced Evidence Decision (establishes, unresolved, impact, relationship) and a 20–400-character same-finding design choice; completing it does not animate a score or correctness state.
- Map nodes expand along the completed path after reflection; next-question branches appear last.
- Target most transitions around 150–300ms; the final reveal may be slightly longer if still responsive.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: remove travel/stagger and reveal the final state immediately without losing information.
- No confetti, bouncing counters, fake streaks, or decorative perpetual animation.

## Accessibility requirements

- Semantic headings follow document order.
- Every control is keyboard reachable with a visible focus indicator.
- Selection is exposed through native/ARIA state, not color alone.
- The Evidence Decision and Evidence → design fields use associated labels, fieldset/legend radio group, character guidance, and validation relationships that remain understandable by keyboard and screen reader.
- Status and error changes use appropriate live regions without excessive announcements.
- External links identify themselves accessibly.
- Map has a structured outline representing the same nodes and relationships.
- Touch targets are at least 44px and controls do not depend on hover.
- Test zoom at 200%, reduced motion, forced colors/high contrast where practical, and mobile reflow.
- Generated strings have length bounds to avoid overflow and inaccessible truncation.

## Voice and microcopy

ReasonWeave speaks to a capable learner. Copy is concise, invitational, specific, and honest. Its promise is direct: **make reasoning visible**.

Use:

- `Choose the route that makes you want to investigate—not the one that sounds easiest.`
- `Make your best first model. You can revise it after seeing evidence.`
- `Evidence could not be verified. Try again or open the complete pre-generated demo.`

Avoid:

- `Great job! You're a natural scientist!`
- `Wrong—try again.`
- `The AI knows…`
- `This will improve your learning.`
- policy labels or provider error codes in learner-facing copy.
