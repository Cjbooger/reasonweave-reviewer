# Seeded usability walkthrough

This is a lightweight protocol for collecting honest product feedback before submission. It is not a learning-efficacy study, and an empty results section means the walkthrough has **not** been run.

## Goal

Observe whether ReasonWeave's core promise—**make reasoning visible**—and learner-owned workflow are understandable without coaching:

1. choose an investigation method;
2. predict before seeing evidence;
3. distinguish Evidence, Inference, and Open Question;
4. select one sourced finding, review its exact linked sources, state what they establish and where their source scope stops, explain the impact, and decide whether the finding supports, challenges, or complicates the prediction;
5. apply that same finding to one concrete design choice;
6. create and self-review a response;
7. reflect in the learner's own words;
8. interpret and export the final Learning Trace.

## Participants and privacy

Target two or three voluntary walkthroughs: ideally two learners age 13+ and one educator, parent, or learning-design practitioner. For anyone under 18, obtain appropriate adult consent before observing or recording anything.

- Use the labeled seeded demo; do not require an account or API key.
- Do not collect names, school, contact details, health information, or other sensitive data.
- Record only anonymous role bands such as `high-school learner`, `college learner`, or `educator`.
- Ask permission before screen/audio recording. Written notes are sufficient.
- Do not describe opinions or task completion as proof of better grades, retention, curiosity, or learning.

## Ten-minute script

1. Show the Spark page for ten seconds. Ask: “What do you think this product will ask you to do?”
2. Open the complete demo and ask the participant to choose the route they find most interesting.
3. Ask them to continue without coaching. If they become blocked, note the screen and exact visible wording before helping.
4. At the Evidence Lens, ask: “Which statements are sourced facts, which are reasoning, and what remains unsettled?”
5. Without suggesting an answer, ask the participant to complete **Your evidence decision**. Observe whether they can choose a sourced finding, use the complete linked source list, understand supports/challenges/complicates, separate what those sources directly establish from inference or what they cannot answer, explain impact in their own words, and recognize that it is not an AI grade.
6. At Create, ask them to complete **Evidence → design**. Observe whether they understand that its 20–400-character concrete design choice must reuse the selected finding, and whether changing evidence makes the cleared link/self-check understandable.
7. Observe whether the constraints and learner self-check make sense and whether the completed evidence-to-design chain remains a useful bridge from prediction to creation.
8. At Discovery, first ask them to explain the compact **At a glance** summary and identify the exact sources and source-scope boundary. Then ask them to open **Full learning trace**, explain the evidence-decision/evidence-to-design map trace, and identify which words are theirs versus ReasonWeave's response. Confirm that they can still find the facilitator prompt and export actions without opening the disclosure.
9. Ask whether they would save/share the card and with whom, without promising that the product improves learning.

## Exit questions

- What felt most useful?
- Where did you hesitate or feel confused?
- Did the product do too much thinking for you, too little, or about the right amount?
- Did the citations and Evidence/Inference/Open Question labels change how you judged the content?
- Was it clear how to choose one sourced finding, use its linked sources to separate what they directly support from inference or an unanswered question, decide the finding's relationship to your prediction, and use it in a concrete design choice? Why?
- Does the final card represent your thinking accurately enough to revisit or share?
- What is the single change you would make before another learner uses it?

## Interpretation

Report observations, not outcomes. Useful submission language looks like: “Two of three participants completed the Evidence Decision and Evidence → design link without coaching; one needed clearer wording for ‘complicates.’” Avoid causal or educational-efficacy claims.

Prioritize a change before recording if the same core-flow friction appears twice, anyone mistakes seeded output for a live call, anyone cannot identify the Evidence Decision or design link as their own judgment, or anyone cannot distinguish their reflection from ReasonWeave's response.
